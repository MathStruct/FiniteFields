overview

Planned work, by section, and the ledger of open sorrys. A row moves to Done with the commit that completes it. Rows were proposed in the planning phase (2026-11-19); the maintainer approves sections for implementation (so far: J0, the nimber fields, J1a, J2a, J3, J10, R1, R2, R3, J2b, J8, J8b, J8c, J8d, J8e, J8g, J9, FF-P2); Status.md says which.

Sections: V vault content, T tooling and site, R research, L Lean, J Julia. Characteristic 2 comes first in every section.

V — Vault content

  • V0 — Restructure the vault. Folders by subject (proposal: Fields/, Characteristic 2/, Prime Fields/, Extension Fields/, Linear Algebra/, Analysis/, Category Theory/, Comparisons/, Algorithms/), a Map of Content.md, a concept-note template in vault/templates/, by-name wikilinks.
  • V1 — Foundations. One note each: field; characteristic; prime field; Fp​ as ZMod p; finite field; order is a prime power; existence and uniqueness of Fpn​ (GaloisField); multiplicative group is cyclic; primitive element; Frobenius; subfield lattice ↔ divisibility of ; Galois group is cyclic generated by Frobenius; trace and norm; minimal polynomial; irreducible polynomials and counting them; Conway polynomials.
  • V2 — Characteristic 2 (priority). F2​ and Boolean arithmetic; and addition is XOR; polynomial basis of F2n​; every element is a square and the square-root map; Artin–Schreier equation and the trace criterion; normal bases; tower fields; quadratic forms in characteristic 2 (symmetric ≠ alternating, Arf invariant); standard fields F28​ (AES), F2128​ (GHASH); examples worked by hand.
  • V3 — Categorical view. Fields have no products and no initial object; the category of finite fields of characteristic and embeddings (hom-sets nonempty iff m∣n, then of size ); Fp​ as a colimit; absolute Galois group Z^; finite étale algebras ↔ finite continuous Z^-sets (Galois categories, Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Galois/); Frobenius as a natural transformation; ModuleCat over Fq​.
  • V4 — Comparisons with R and C. No ordering; only the trivial absolute value; discrete topology and no limits; scalar products: no InnerProductSpace, but bilinear/hermitian forms, isotropic vectors, failure of Gram–Schmidt, orthogonal complements that meet the space; differentiability: formal derivative, , Hasse derivatives and Taylor expansion in characteristic ; Fourier analysis via additive characters (Walsh–Hadamard in characteristic 2); what “analysis over Fq​” means (scope of FF-004).
  • V6 — Nimbers and Conway polynomials: nim-addition and nim-multiplication; the nimber fields F22k​ and the quadratic closure; Conway polynomials and their compatibility as a functor on the divisibility poset; pseudo-Conway polynomials; changes of representation as linear maps. Lean: CombinatorialGames’ Nimber (check what it proves before relying on it).
  • V7 — Learners over finite fields (the maintainer’s experiment): what replaces gradients (discrete optimisation, interpolation, Reed–Muller, learning parity with noise), once the maintainer has written the ideas down.
  • V5 — Linear algebra over Fq​ (was FF-003): counting subspaces, GLn​(Fq​) order, rank, solving; specification before code.

T — Tooling and site

  • T1 — Snippet sync tool. Takes a declaration name, asks Lean (against the pinned mathlib) for signature, docstring and module, and writes a > [!mathlib] callout between markers (…) with a link to the mathlib docs and an attribution line (mathlib is Apache-2.0). Plain Markdown, so Obsidian and Quartz both render it; a re-run refreshes it; a renamed declaration fails loudly. The same tool syncs our own Lean and Julia snippets from anchors in the sources. Caveat: the hosted mathlib docs follow master, not our pin. A Quartz plugin would not render in Obsidian, hence the generator.
  • T2 — Lean API docs with doc-gen4 (the tool behind mathlib’s docs), published under a subpath (e.g. <site>/lean/) beside the vault, as Lenticulum.jl publishes Documenter under api/; Julia API docs (Documenter) likewise under <site>/julia/. First check whether doc-gen4 can be limited to our modules: by default it documents every import, i.e. much of mathlib.
  • T3 — Note checker (Lenticulum’s check_notes.py as model): dangling wikilinks, missing tag lines, missing sources, unlisted sorrys.
  • T4 — CI, remaining: a benchmark job (on demand, not per push) once bench/ exists; a job that fails on an unlisted sorry (with T3).
  • T7 — Register FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl in the General registry (subdir=julia/FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl) once it exists and has a first release.

R — Research

  • R3 — Lean: tower digits = binary digits (DiMuro’s Lemma 1.1) (approved 2026-11-21: “lean proof approved! Add it to the backlog.”). The one unformalized link between the R1 data (ranks computed with tower digits: monomials in the χr​ times finite nimber bits) and nimSum_lt_of_rank_lt (binary digits). On CombinatorialGames’ Nimber:
    1. one step: CombinatorialGames’ IsRing.eval_eq_of_lt already says that for a ring with X ^ n ≤ leastNoRoots x and of degree with coefficients , the nim value is the ordinal oeval x p =[xn−1an−1​+⋯+a0​] (this is Lemma 1.1);
    2. nesting: iterate it over a tower g1​<⋯<gk​ to get the ordinal of ∑e​gkek​​⋯g1e1​​ce​, and prove that the ordinal order on such elements is lexicographic in (monomial, then ), i.e. that the colex order on tower digits equals digits_strictMono’s order on binary digits (a statement about ordinal arithmetic: Cantor normal form base versus base 2);
    3. conclude: for a finite subfield given in a tower, the reduced echelon basis for tower digits is the one for binary digits, so nimSum_lt_of_rank_lt applies to the R1 ranks. Hypotheses of step 1 for a concrete (that is a field and the degree of leastNoRoots x) are inputs here, not proved: for x=ω that is exactly the proof_wanted leastNoRoots_omega0 (ω3=2) of CombinatorialGames. Proving the concrete relations (Theorem 3.1, Lenstra’s αu​, R1’s 17 new values) is a separate, much larger task, not part of R3 (not approved).

J — Julia (FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl)

  • J10 — Rank-encoded fields, remaining (approved 2026-11-21; the type is done, see Done): faster minimal_degree/hash (Frobenius-power byte tables per maximal divisor; now 1.7 µs at ); bulk operations (MulBy, matrix products, bitsliced vectors) for Nim, converting once at the boundary; embed between Nim and the Conway fields (now possible canonically: the Conway generator is an element of On2​).

  • J1a — Representations, remaining (approved 2026-11-21; first part done, see Done): nimbers for , Conway polynomials for every (pseudo-Conway beyond the tables), standard polynomials (AES, GHASH, …); selected by a type parameter; conversion maps between them. Decided (2026-11-19): nimbers default, Conway secondary, the others alternatives. Printing always hex; the REPL adds the field when it does not fill its storage. Open:

    • pseudo-Conway polynomials for the degrees missing from Lübeck’s table ( and 14 others below it), compatible with the table; not canonical, so the construction needs the maintainer’s choice;
    • isomorphisms between AES/GHASH and the Conway fields (there are ; which one, or all);
    • Conway → Conway embeddings by byte tables per pair (now 335 ns – 1.5 µs per element, the nimber isomorphisms 0.2–1.9 ns);
    • table strategies for small Conway fields (products 2.9 ns against 0.4 for nimber GF(256); inverses 14 ns with Itoh–Tsujii against 0.3);
    • bulk operations (MulBy, matrix products) for PolyElement;
    • whether Conway fields of different degrees should promote (and compare equal) through embed, as nimbers do; that needs hash through the smallest subfield (J9b).

  • J1b — Arbitrary : a function (or macro, if a function cannot give type stability) that constructs F2n​ for any : picks the smallest fitting UInt for n≤128, multi-word storage above; finds an irreducible polynomial (low-weight trinomial or pentanomial search with an irreducibility test) or takes a Conway polynomial from a table.

  • J2 — Multiplication strategies, selected by dispatch and compared by benchmark; both table and computed variants where possible (maintainer, 2026-11-19), small fields probably tables only: shift-and-add, log/antilog tables (small ), carry-less multiplication (x86 PCLMULQDQ, ARM PMULL via LLVM intrinsics), reduction for sparse polynomials; profiled.

  • J3 — Inversion and division, remaining (approved 2026-11-21; strategies done, see Done): division without a separate inverse (Euclid started at gives directly; / is still x * inv(y)); a faster Euclid (branch-free, or several quotient bits per step); bulk inversion (Montgomery’s trick: products and one inverse for elements).

  • J4 — Fp​ in the smallest fitting UInt; Barrett/Montgomery as options.

  • J5 — Fpn​ generic.

  • J6 — Constant time: not a goal. Document which strategies happen to be constant-time; do not engineer for it.

  • J8b — Bulk, remaining (after J8; approved 2026-11-20; packing, the cache-blocking measurement, the bitsliced matrix product and bitsliced vectors at every level done, see Done): ARM PMULL; GPU kernels (needs a GPU to test).

  • J8f — Fewer carry-less products in CLMulMatMul (proposed 2026-11-20, not approved): the matrix product is bound by PCLMULQDQ throughput, not by the caches (src/bulk.md, “Larger sizes and cache blocking”). Two terms per 64-bit product at GF(2^16) ( times ); VPCLMULQDQ (four products per AVX-512 instruction) at every level. Tests and benchmark first, as usual.

  • J7 — F2​ ("n=∞"): every element lives in some finite F2n​, so elements carry their degree and an operation embeds both operands into F2lcm(m,n)​ through Conway polynomials (pseudo-Conway beyond the tables). Exact, not fast; sizes grow with the lcm. The nimber quadratic closure (all ) is the cheap special case. After J1–J3. Lean counterpart: AlgebraicClosure (ZMod 2).

  • J9b — Smallest field for the Conway representation and the others (requested by the maintainer 2026-11-20, to be implemented with or after J1a/J1b; F2​ after J7; the nimber part, J9, is done): for an element x of a Conway field F2k​ (any , not only powers of two), the caller can ask for either

    • the integer minimal_degree(x) =d=[F2​(x):F2​], the smallest d∣k with (type-stable), or
    • the smallest containing field type: reduce_field(x), x as an element of the Conway field F2d​ (its type depends on the value, so type-unstable as for nimbers), through the Conway compatibility: is written in powers of β=αk(2k−1)/(2d−1)​, which the compatibility of Conway polynomials identifies with the generator αd​ of F2d​.

    minimal_level has no Conway counterpart unless is a power of two. The divisors of can be tried from the smallest (or the largest proper ones ruled out first); measure which. Tests first, with the Frobenius oracle of test/subfield.jl; for , also cross-checked against the nimber results through the isomorphism of J1a.

  • J8b — Why inlining both LazyMulBy branches slowed the loop (2026-11-20): LLVM’s loop unswitching cost multiplier grows with the loops in a method, so with both loops inlined the scalar loop kept a per-element broadcast index select (8–10% at GF(2^16)); confirmed with --enable-unswitch-cost-multiplier=false. The function barrier stays. bench/constmul_inline.jl; src/bulk.md.
  • J8g — BitslicedMatMul as the default by size (approved and done 2026-11-20): default_matmul_strategy(F) is BySizeMatMul(previous default, BitslicedMatMul(), t), the bitsliced product when every dimension is at least t = bitsliced_threshold(F): 48 (GF(2), GF(4)), 64, 96, 192, 256, 512 (GF(2^64)), 384 (GF(2^128)), from bench/matmul_threshold.jl; rectangular shapes checked. Details in src/bitsliced_matmul.md, “Default by size”. Found on the way: FF-P2 (CLMulMatMul slow for matrix × vector).
  • J8b, bitsliced matrix product and bitsliced vectors at every level (2026-11-20): BitslicedMatMul (the nimber recursion on bit matrices, GF(2) products by the Four Russians), 48–170× faster at GF(2) … GF(256) and 2–13× above from 1024 × 1024; the default from a size per field since J8g. BitslicedVector at GF(2^64) and GF(2^128) (top recursion levels through a scratch buffer): 7.1× and 5.6× the scalar product. Details in src/bitsliced_matmul.md and src/bulk.md.

Reverted; the code is in git (79a7ab8, 20b8cba). A larger-size test added. Details in src/bulk.md, “Larger sizes and cache blocking”.
  • J8b, first part — BitslicedVector packing by bit-matrix transposes (2026-11-20): delta swaps on tuples of words (transpose64! for the full 64×64 case); pack 0.09–1.4 ns and unpack 0.10–0.83 ns per element, 3.8–7.1 times faster from GF(16) up (bench/pack.jl). Details in src/bulk.md, “Packing by bit-matrix transposes”.
  • J8 — Bulk and array operations (2026-11-19): fast rand! for arrays, broadcasting (tested, no code needed), MulBy (multiplication by a constant as byte tables), matrix products (ScalarMatMul, TableMatMul, CLMulMatMul with one reduction per entry; default CLMulMatMul from GF(2^16), 3.5 ns per multiply-add at GF(2^128) against 79), * and LinearAlgebra.mul!, BitslicedVector up to GF(2^32); isbits element types, GPU not tested.
