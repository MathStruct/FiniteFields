ADR 0004 Nimbers as the default representation of GF(2^n)

ADR-0004 Nimbers as the default representation of GF(2^n)

Nov 21, 20263 min read

  • design
  • characteristic-2

design characteristic-2

Status: accepted (2026-11-19, maintainer); the part “Conway polynomials … the default for every other ” is superseded by ADR-0005 Rank encoding of GF(2^n) in Conway’s On2 (2026-11-21). Elements of F22k​ are nimbers by default; Conway polynomials are the secondary representation and the default for every other ; the cryptographers’ polynomials (AES, GHASH, …) are alternatives for cryptanalysis and interoperability.

Code: julia/FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl/src/nimber.jl (and its note nimber.md). Backlog: J1a, J1b, J7, V6.

Context

A field F2n​ has many concrete representations, one per choice of basis; the usual one is a polynomial basis modulo an irreducible polynomial of degree . The maintainer asked for the choice that gives the most structure, especially across several fields, rather than a polynomial picked by cryptographers for cheap reduction. The project’s purpose (general theory, cryptanalysis, learners built on finite fields) favours structure and bulk arithmetic over interoperability.

Options considered

  1. Conway polynomials , for every : the lexicographically least monic primitive polynomial of degree over F2​ compatible with for all m∣n, i.e. Cm​(x(2n−1)/(2m−1))≡0(modCn​). Then the embeddings F2m​↪F2n​, αm​↦αn(2n−1)/(2m−1)​, commute. Categorically: finite fields of characteristic 2 have embeddings F2m​→F2n​ when m∣n (one per Galois automorphism); Conway polynomials choose one coherently, a functor from the divisibility poset (N>0​,∣) into fields whose colimit is a concrete F2​. Since αn​ is primitive, discrete logarithms are compatible as well. Costs: “least” is a convention (the compatibility is the structure); the tables (Lübeck) end at some degree, beyond which pseudo-Conway polynomials (compatible, not minimal) must be computed; they are dense, so reduction is slower than with a trinomial.
  2. Nimbers (Conway, On Numbers and Games, ch. 6), for : the integers below with XOR and nim-multiplication form F22k​, and these fields are nested as sets of integers. The embedding keeps the bit pattern; no polynomial is chosen at all (each level is the Artin–Schreier extension by a root of of the one below). The union is the quadratic closure of F2​, and levels 3–7 are exactly the byte-sized fields of 8–128 bits, the maintainer’s priority. Lean: the CombinatorialGames project defines Nimber (on our toolchain v4.36.0); mathlib v4.36.0 has neither nimbers nor Conway polynomials. Cost: no hardware instruction like carry-less multiplication helps; the large levels must be benchmarked against polynomial bases.
  3. Standard low-weight polynomials (AES , GHASH ): fast reduction and interoperability with existing software; no structure across fields.

Decision

Nimbers for , Conway polynomials (pseudo-Conway beyond the tables) for other and for F2​, standard polynomials as alternatives. A change of representation is an F2​-linear map (an n×n bit matrix), cheap in bulk.

Consequences

  • Subfield arithmetic is free in the nimber representation: GF(16) values are GF(256) values, so mixed-level arithmetic is promotion, and one set of GF(256) tables serves every level up to it (measured: nimber.md).
  • Conversions between representations must be explicit (no implicit isomorphism); elements of different representations compare unequal.
  • Fields that are not of the form (e.g. F27​) wait for the Conway representation (J1b).
