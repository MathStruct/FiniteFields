FF-P1 — CLMul at GF(2^64) loses its speed in the element wrapper (open, 2026-11-19)

  • Where: julia/FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl/src/nimber_fast.jl, nim_mul_bits(::CLMul, …, Val(6)) and mul(CLMul(), a::GF2_64, b::GF2_64); measurements in src/nimber_fast.md.
  • Reproduction: broadcast over 4096 random pairs, in one process. On UInt64 bit patterns, nim_mul_bits(CLMul(), x, y, Val(6)) takes 10.4–11.7 ns per product against 18–20 ns for MulTable. Wrapped as GF2_64 (mul(CLMul(), a, b) or unsafe_frombits(GF2_64, nim_mul_bits(…))) it takes 17–19 ns. The native code of both functions is nearly identical (168 vs 179 instructions, the same single out-of-line call).
  • Expected: the wrapper (an isbits struct around the UInt64) costs nothing.
  • Actual: about 7 ns per product in some runs. The VM’s run-to-run noise (up to about 40%) makes it unclear how much is real: one run of the bare variant also took 18 ns.
  • Consequence: GF(2^64) keeps MulTable as default. Investigate on a quiet machine (code_llvm of the whole broadcast loop, perf), then reconsider the default.

FF-P2 — CLMulMatMul is slow for matrix × vector (resolved, 2026-11-20)

  • Where: julia/FiniteFieldArithmetic.jl/src/bulk.jl, matmul_add!(::CLMulMatMul, …), the default for A * x above GF(256) (below the bitsliced threshold, which a vector never reaches).
  • Reproduction: bench/matmul_threshold.jl, rows … × 1: 1024×1024 times a vector takes 9.0 ns per multiply-add at GF(2^32), 17.5 at GF(2^64), 70 at GF(2^128), against 0.3, 0.6 and 3 per multiply-add for square products.
  • Expected: about the cost of a square product per multiply-add.
  • Actual: the conversion of all of to the polynomial basis (and transposed) is paid for one column; for a vector it costs more than the products. (An explanation from the code, not profiled.)
  • Consequence: none for correctness. Options: a matrix × vector path without converting (scalar CLMul products, or MulBy-style tables of the vector entries), chosen by .
  • Resolution (2026-11-20): a column-wise path without the transpose (clmul_columns_add!), chosen by clmul_by_columns(F, m, p, q): a vector with A≥ 512 KiB, two columns with A≥ 4 MiB and m,p≥256. The measurement showed the crossover depends on the size of , not only on : for small the transposed path is as fast or faster even for a vector. 1024×1024 times a vector now 1.05 / 2.94 / 14.9 ns per multiply-add at GF(2^32) / GF(2^64) / GF(2^128). Details and bench/matvec.jl in src/bulk.md, “Matrix × vector”.
