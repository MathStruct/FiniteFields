Done last

  • R2 on CombinatorialGames’ Nimber (lean/FiniteFields/NimberDigits.lean): binary digits of ordinals; nim-addition = symmetric difference of digits, ordinal order = colex of digits, and the rank encoding is order-preserving for any reduced echelon family of nimbers (nimSum_lt_of_rank_lt); no sorry, standard axioms only, lake build passes. New Lean dependency CombinatorialGames 061f2c9 (last commit on v4.36.0; builds against our mathlib tag). Policy recorded: other Lean libraries before own definitions (AGENTS.md, Lean and mathlib). Not formalized: tower digits = binary digits (DiMuro Lemma 1.1); existence of echelon bases. Not verified: the Lean CI run (lean.yml) with the new dependency.

  • J10: nim_field(n) for every n≤128 (src/nimfield.jl, note src/nimfield.md, data src/nim_data.jl generated from R1, tests test/nimfield.jl, benchmark bench/nimfield.jl): Nimber for , Nim{n, T} otherwise, elements stored as ranks; products 3.6–58 ns (Conway + three byte-table maps); embed, ==, hash, promotion across all degrees through On2​; minimal_degree/reduce_field. Verified: Pkg.test() passes (2 622 552 checks, Aqua, JET), including agreement with the R1 data for every degree.

  • R2: the order-preservation proof (Rank encoding is order-preserving) and its combinatorial step in Lean (lean/FiniteFields/RankEncoding.lean, no sorry; lake build passes). Not formalized: the digit structure of On2​ (DiMuro Lemma 1.1).

  • Not verified: CI (lean.yml, julia.yml, Full CI), other Julia versions; the jldoctests.

  • R1 and ADR-0005: the rank encoding of every F2n​⊂On2​ (ADR-0005 Rank encoding of GF(2^n) in Conway’s On2, accepted; results in Canonical extension of the nimbers, “Results of R1”; code and data in research/nimber_closure/). Lenstra’s αu​ computed for every prime u≤127 (the 13 of his table reproduced from scratch, 17 new, e.g. α47​=χ23​+1, α107​=χ53​+1); rank-encoding data (internal generator, minimal polynomial, change of basis, all inclusions) for n=1…128 (, tower degree 884 676, took an hour). Checks pass: Lenstra’s table, base-field independence, irreducibility, rank = nimber for , inclusions are compatible ring homomorphisms, and rank = ordinal position by brute force for 13 small fields. Not done: the order-preservation proof is a sketch (R2); no library type yet (J10); nothing in Lean. The package itself is unchanged.

  • Research: a canonical extension of the nimbers to every F2n​ (maintainer 2026-11-21: rejected StandardFF for its pseudo-random choices, asked for a canonical structure; “Do that!”). Note Canonical extension of the nimbers, scripts in research/nimber_closure/. Result: Conway’s On2​ below ωωω (DiMuro, arXiv:1108.0962, after Lenstra 1977); Lenstra’s table of αu​ (u≤43) verified with our nimber arithmetic; only F22k​ are initial segments, but the rank of an element in ordinal order is a canonical, linear -bit encoding equal to the nimber value for . Needed and unknown: the excesses for the 17 primes 47 … 127. Nothing in the library changed.

  • J3, inversion strategies (src/inversion.jl, note src/inversion.md, tests test/inversion.jl, benchmark bench/inversion.jl): Fermat(), ItohTsujii() (Frobenius powers as byte tables built per field at compile time, an unrolled @generated chain), Euclid() (first step removes , so no wider type and also degree 128; nimbers through the CLMul basis from GF(2^16) up). Default inv for polynomial bases is now ItohTsujii (2–3× Euclid; GHASH 3.1 µs → 340 ns); nimber defaults unchanged (faster than all three). Verified: Pkg.test() passes, JET @test_opt on all strategies. Not verified: CI, other Julia versions; the two explanations of the timings in the note are labelled unprofiled.

  • J1a, first part: polynomial-basis and Conway fields (src/polyfield.jl, note src/polyfield.md, data src/conway_table.jl, tests test/conway.jl, benchmark bench/polyfield.jl). PolyElement{N, T, F} = F2​[x]/(xN+F) for N≤128; poly_field(n, low); conway_field(n) for the 113 degrees ≤128 in Lübeck’s table (not 128); GF256_AES, GF2_128_GHASH; embed between Conway fields (m∣n) and between Conway and nimber fields of degree 2k≤64. Products: carry-less multiplication plus Barrett reduction (exactness proved in the note). Inverses: extended Euclid (Fermat at 128). A small refactor: PolyBasis(F, α) in src/nimber_fast.jl takes the root.

  • Measured (src/polyfield.md): Conway products 2.5–3 ns up to degree 64, 9 ns above; faster than the nimber defaults from GF(2^32) up (2.5 vs 18.9 ns at 64; GHASH 8.4 vs 40.5 at 128), slower below (2.9 vs 0.4 ns at GF(256)). Inverses slow (400 ns at 64, 3 µs GHASH). Nimber ↔ Conway 0.2–1.9 ns per element; Conway → Conway 335 ns – 1.5 µs.

  • Verified: Pkg.test() passes (Julia 1.13.1, x86_64-linux), incl. Aqua and JET (@test_opt on products/inverses of six polynomial fields). Table: every entry irreducible and compatible with its divisors, primitive for n≤64, the least such polynomial for n≤16 (independent BigInt oracle). Products against the oracle on every Conway field, exhaustive up to ; FIPS-197 AES vectors.

  • Not verified: primitivity of the table entries above degree 64 (needs factorisations of ); a GCM test vector for GF2_128_GHASH (bit order differs, documented); other Julia versions; CI; the jldoctest of conway_field (no Documenter run).

Before that: J9 (smallest field of a nimber), FF-P2 (CLMulMatMul for matrix × vector), J8b (inlining LazyMulBy), J8g (BitslicedMatMul the default by size), the bitsliced matrix product and vectors at GF(2^64)/GF(2^128). Earlier: J8b cache blocking (measured, not adopted), J8b packing by bit-matrix transposes, J2b, J8e and J8d, J8c, J8, J2a, nimber GF(2) … GF(2^128), ADR-0004, AGENTS.md, CI, the move to MathStruct. Decisions in force (maintainer, 2026-11-19)
