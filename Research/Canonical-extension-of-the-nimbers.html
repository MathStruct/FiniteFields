Research note (2026-11-21), asked by the maintainer: is there a canonical structure on all finite fields F2n​ that extends the nimbers, with nesting and embeddings, but no arbitrary choices (no polynomial tables, no pseudo-random searches)? Answer: yes, Conway’s field On2​ below ωωω. Only the F22k​ are initial segments there; for other the elements are large ordinals, but numbering the elements of F2n​ by their rank in ordinal order gives a canonical, F2​-linear, -bit encoding that is the nimber value when . R1 (below) computed all of it for n≤128: Lenstra’s αu​ for every prime u≤127 (17 of them new) and the rank-encoding data.

The structure of On2​ below ωωω

Conway defines nim-addition and nim-multiplication on all ordinals by the minimal-excludant recursion (the same definition that gives our finite nimbers), and the result is an algebraically closed field. Every choice is forced by the definition: at each step the next field is the extension by a root of the lexicographically earliest polynomial without a root (DiMuro §1.1; the simplest extension theorem, formalized in CombinatorialGames). So whatever follows is canonical in exactly the sense in which the nimbers already are.

DiMuro, Theorem 3.1 (for ), with χr​ for prime powers : χ2n​=[22n−1] (the finite nimber fields), and for the -th odd prime , χun​=[ωωk−1un−1], e.g. χ3​=ω, χ9​=[ω3], χ5​=[ωω], χ7​=[ωω2].

  1. [ωωω] is the first transcendental; the ordinals below it that are fields are exactly the χr​.
  2. χun​ is a degree- extension of χun−1​ (and closed under extensions of degree ).
  3. For odd : χuu​=αu​, the smallest ordinal in χu​ with no -th root in χu​; and χun+1u​=χun​ for n≥1.
  4. For : the Artin–Schreier tower of our nimbers.

Theorem 4.6: αu​=[χf(u)​+m] with f(u)=ordu​2 and a natural number , the excess; χh​ for any is a sum of χr​ given by Theorem 4.3. Lemma 1.1: in a degree- step φ~​⊃φ, the ordinal [∑φiαi​] equals the nim-expression ∑φiαi​, so elements are nested radical-extension polynomials with finite nimbers at the bottom.

Finding 2: a canonical -bit encoding, the rank in ordinal order

By Lemma 1.1 and induction, below ωωω every ordinal is a finite set of “digits” (monomials ∏χrer​​ times a power of two of the finite level), nim-addition is the symmetric difference (XOR) of digit sets, and the ordinal order compares the highest differing digit. F2n​ is an -dimensional F2​-subspace. Its reduced echelon basis bn−1​>⋯>b0​ (distinct leading digits, no basis vector containing another’s leading digit) makes the map

rank:F2n​→{0,…,2n−1},∑i​εi​bi​↦∑i​εi​2i

order-preserving (the leading digits decide) and F2​-linear. So: number the elements of F2n​ by their position in ordinal order. (Sketch of proof; the order-preservation is the standard argument for echelon bases, to be written out properly.)

  • No choice is made anywhere: the ordinals, the field, and the order are Conway’s.
  • For , F2n​ is the initial segment of ordinals below , i.e. the integers below , and rank = value: exactly our Nimber.
  • F8​ above: ranks 0 … 7 in the listed order; e.g. rank 2 ⊕ rank 4 = rank 6 ✓.
  • Embeddings F2m​↪F2n​ for m∣n are the inclusions inside On2​: canonical, compatible (a functor from the divisibility poset, colimit =ωωω itself), and in rank coordinates a fixed m×n bit matrix. Equality and hashing across fields can go through the ordinal (or through the smallest subfield), as with nimbers.
  • Arithmetic in rank coordinates: through any internal basis (e.g. a polynomial basis with carry-less products, as CLMul does for nimbers) and an n×n change of basis. The internal basis is invisible, so it is an implementation choice, not a convention of the structure.
  • The runtime data per is small (n×n bits, plus a modulus and the embedding images); the work is computing it once.

Categorically: this is the canonical choice of one embedding per pair m∣n that Conway polynomials provide by a table, here provided by On2​; with rank coordinates each F2n​ also gets a canonical F2​-basis, which Conway polynomials do not give (they give a canonical generator).

The rank encoding for n=1…128

For each : the tower (base nimber field containing F22-part​ and the relation scalars, odd generators for the odd prime powers of ), a spanning set of F2n​ (products of the nimber bits and the Frobenius conjugates of a normal element TrLn​/F2r​​(y) for each odd prime power ), its reduced echelon basis by the ordinal order, and from it, in rank coordinates: an internal generator (the first basis element of degree ), its minimal polynomial, the powers , and the inclusions of every F2m​, m∣n. Data: data/rank/n_*.txt (one file per ; these are the inputs of the library type). The random and the spanning set do not affect any rank; only fixes the internal basis.

Largest towers: (degree 884 676; 3 629 s, about an hour, single core), (237 820; 107 s, 187 s), (143 724; 116 s, 118 s); all other under 4 s each.

Checks (check_rank_data.jl, all pass):

  1. every minimal polynomial is irreducible (Rabin, via the package’s poly_field);
  2. for (all eight), products in rank coordinates equal Nimber products;
  3. every inclusion F2m​⊂F2n​ is an injective ring homomorphism, and inclusions compose along every chain l∣m∣n;
  4. F8​⊂F64​ matches gf8_as_ordinals.jl;
  5. rank = position in ordinal order, by brute force: for every whose tower has degree ≤20 (13 fields, up to elements), all elements of F2n​ are enumerated in the tower and sorted as ordinals; the -th is the element of rank . This checks Finding 2 (order-preservation and linearity) directly on those fields.

Not checked: the order-preservation proof is still a sketch (check 5 covers small fields only); nothing is formalized in Lean; Lenstra’s 1977 paper itself was not read.
