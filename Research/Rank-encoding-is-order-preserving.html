Ordinal. Order. Ordinals of the form [φu−1αu−1​+⋯+α0​] with all αi​<φ are compared by the highest with αi​=αi′​, then by αi​ (the base-φ Cantor normal form). Inductively, two elements of compare by the largest monomial gkek​​⋯g1e1​​ (exponent of first, then , …) in which their coefficients differ, and then by those coefficients, which are finite nimbers, i.e. integers, compared by their highest differing bit.

Digits. Call a pair (monomial, bit , ) a digit, ordered as just described (monomial first, then bit). Then x↦ (its set of digits with coefficient bit 1) identifies with the finite subsets of the digits, the ordinal order with the colexicographic order (compare the largest digit in the symmetric difference), and nim-addition with the symmetric difference (addition is coefficientwise, DiMuro’s map θ is additive, and finite nim-addition is XOR).

Independence of the tower. A larger tower L′⊇L (more generators, larger base) adds digits but keeps the relative order of the old ones, so the order of two elements does not depend on the tower used. Hence ranks are absolute: they depend only on the subfield.

Step 1 in Lean: binary digits instead of tower digits

The formal proof uses a simpler digit structure than the tower of step 1, valid on all ordinals and needing no description of On2​ at all: the binary Cantor normal form o=2e1​+2e2​+… (e1​>e2​>…), digits o ={e1​,e2​,…}.

  • digits_add: nim-addition is the symmetric difference of binary digits. Proof: every nimber is the nim-sum ∑e∈digits​∗2e (by Nimber.two_opow_log_add), and for the nim-sum is the ordinal (IsGroup.add_eq_of_lt, since is a group, IsGroup.two_opow); in characteristic 2 the common digits cancel.
  • digits_strictMono: the ordinal order is colex on binary digits (compare log2​, then the remainders, by well-founded induction).
  • nimSum_lt_of_rank_lt: for any nimbers forming a reduced echelon family for their binary digits, ∑i∈e​bi​ increases strictly with ∑i∈e​2i.

So the theorem holds for every finite F2​-subspace of the nimbers, in particular every finite subfield, with “digit” meaning binary digit. The R1 computation used tower digits (monomials times nimber bits, step 1 above); that both give the same order is DiMuro’s Lemma 1.1, which stays the one unformalized link between the computed data and the Lean theorem (it is also what check 5 of check_rank_data.jl tests by brute force). Not formalized either: the existence of a reduced echelon basis (standard linear algebra).

Remarks

Comparison with R, C: there is no analogue: an ordered field has no finite subfields, and here the order is not a field order (it is compatible neither with multiplication nor with translation x↦x+c); the lemma says only that rank coordinates enumerate the subfield in increasing order.
